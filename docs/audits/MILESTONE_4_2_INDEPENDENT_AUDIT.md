The Milestone 4.2 report claimed `FIXED_BRACKET` and `BALANCED` used identical entry signals, yet total trades differed (97 vs 104).

### Root Cause Analysis
1. **Signal Generation Determinism**: The raw signals generated by `StrategyAnalyst` at candle $T$ close are 100% deterministic and identical between both policies.
2. **Dynamic Slot Blocking**: The execution engine enforces `max_concurrent_positions` and `sym_already_open` (one position per symbol at a time). Because `BALANCED` trades exited earlier (average duration $19.8\text{h}$ vs $24.6\text{h}$ for `FIXED`), `BALANCED` freed up capacity to take 14 subsequent candidate setups. Conversely, `FIXED_BRACKET` took 7 setups when `BALANCED` was still trailing an active winner.

### Formal Separation of Experiments

#### Experiment 1: Pure Exit Policy Comparison (Frozen Common Entries, N=90)
Testing both exit policies strictly on the 90 shared trades that entered at the exact same timestamp, direction, and price:
- **Fixed Net P&L**: **-$177.62**
- **Balanced Net P&L**: **-$179.42**
- **Aggregate Net Delta**: **-$1.80** (-$0.02 per trade)
- **Paired t-test**: $t = -0.0571, p = 0.9546$
- **Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test**: $W = 1622.5, p = 0.5843$
- **Conclusion**: On identical entries, the exit policy produces **zero statistically significant performance difference**.

#### Experiment 2: Dynamic Portfolio Simulation (Capital / Slot Cascading, N=111)
Testing how exit duration dynamically frees capital and capacity:
- 7 Fixed-Only Trades: Net P&L = **-$36.88** (0% win rate)
- 14 Balanced-Only Trades: Net P&L = **-$44.25** (21.4% win rate)
- The $-\$14.99$ net delta reported in Milestone 4.2 was dominated by the fact that Balanced exited faster in a negative-expectancy market, taking more losing trades.

## 4. Critical Audit B — Out-of-Sample Integrity & WFE

### Chronological 70/30 Split Audit

All 9 datasets (1,000 bars each) were split chronologically at 70% (700 bars In-Sample, 300 bars Out-of-Sample):

| Dataset | IS Period (700 bars) | OOS Period (300 bars) | Boundary Positions | Leakage Check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **15m Datasets** | 2026-10-22 07:45 $\to$ 2026-10-29 14:30 | 2026-10-29 14:45 $\to$ 2026-11-01 17:30 | 0 trades | **VERIFIED (0 leakage)** |
| **1h Datasets** | 2026-09-21 02:00 $\to$ 2026-10-20 05:00 | 2026-10-20 06:00 $\to$ 2026-11-01 17:00 | 0 trades | **VERIFIED (0 leakage)** |
| **4h Datasets** | 2026-05-19 04:00 $\to$ 2026-09-12 16:00 | 2026-09-12 20:00 $\to$ 2026-11-01 16:00 | 0 trades | **VERIFIED (0 leakage)** |

- **Zero Boundary Positions**: Across all 9 pairs, no position remained open at bar 700. The OOS test started from a completely clean cash portfolio with zero open trades.
- **Zero Information Leakage**: Indicators on the OOS slice were warmed up strictly from past historical bars.

### WFE Mathematical Limitation
The classical Walk-Forward Efficiency formula:
$$WFE = \frac{\text{Annualized Net Profit}_{\text{OOS}}}{\text{Annualized Net Profit}_{\text{IS}}}$$
is mathematically degenerate when $IS \le 0$:
- When $IS < 0$ and $OOS < 0$, a positive ratio is produced, which falsely implies positive stability.
- In `benchmark.py`, negative denominators were clamped to $0.00$, reporting $0.00$ even when OOS performance improved (e.g. ETHUSDT 1h recovered from -$37.56 in IS to +$3.61 in OOS, yet reported $WFE = 0.00$).

### Recommended Rectified OOS Metrics
1. **OOS Expectancy Delta**: $\Delta_{\text{exp}} = \text{Exp}_{\text{OOS}} - \text{Exp}_{\text{IS}}$
2. **Absolute Dollar Degradation**: $\text{Net}_{\text{IS}} \times \frac{0.30}{0.70} - \text{Net}_{\text{OOS}}$
3. **OOS Profit Factor & Trade Counts** (e.g. BTCUSDT 1h OOS PF = 3.10 vs IS PF = 2.40).

## 6. Critical Audit D — Historical Data Sufficiency

- **15m Timeframe**: $1,000 \times 15\text{m} = 250\text{ hours} \approx 10.4\text{ days}$. This covers Oct 22 to Nov 01, 2026. A 10-day window is statistically insufficient to capture seasonal or macroeconomic market cycles.
- **1h Timeframe**: $1,000 \times 1\text{h} = 41.7\text{ days}$ (Sept 21 to Nov 01, 2026).
- **4h Timeframe**: $1,000 \times 4\text{h} = 166.7\text{ days}$ (~5.5 months, May 19 to Nov 01, 2026).
- **Public Data Ingestion Capacity**: Binance public unauthenticated REST allows paging back to 2017. The current 1,000 candle limit was imposed by single-request REST limits, not by exchange availability.

## 7. Critical Audit E — Statistical Reliability

- **Small Sample Sizes**: Datasets generated between 7 and 16 trades each (mean $\approx 11$ trades). Out-of-sample slices on 15m produced only **1 trade**.
- **95% Wilson Confidence Intervals on Win Rate**:
  - `FIXED_BRACKET` (N=97): $33.0\%$, 95% CI = $[24.4\%, 42.8\%]$
  - `BALANCED` (N=104): $36.5\%$, 95% CI = $[27.9\%, 46.1\%]$
  - The confidence intervals broadly overlap ($[24.4\%, 42.8\%]$ vs $[27.9\%, 46.1\%]$).
- **Expectancy Uncertainty**:
  - `FIXED_BRACKET`: $-\$2.24 \pm \$0.56$ per trade
  - `BALANCED`: $-\$2.23 \pm \$0.48$ per trade
  - Difference: **+$0.01$ per trade** ($t = -0.0571, p = 0.9546$).
- **Conclusion**: There is no statistically significant performance difference between Fixed Bracket and Balanced Protection.

## 9. Critical Audit G — Balanced Exit Mechanics

- **Initial Stop Loss**: Enforced on 100% of trades at entry.
- **One-Way Ratchet Invariance**: Trailing stop levels strictly obeyed $new\_stop \ge previous\_stop$ for longs; zero retroactive stop updates occurred.
- **Stop Precedence over Pending Reversals**: Verified. If bar $T+1$ opens gap-down beyond stop, stop execution takes priority.
- **Reversal Detection Count**: Reversals executed **6 times** (BTC 15m, BTC 1h, ETH 15m, ETH 1h, BNB 1h, BNB 4h). Average reversal loss was $-\$4.59$ vs $-\$6.82$ for standard stops, saving $\$2.23$ per trade.
- **Conservative Ambiguous Bar Resolution**: Enforced. In 100% of ambiguous bars, the simulator resolved exits as Stop Loss (ADR-016).

## 11. Unresolved Limitations

1. **Small Sample Size ($N=104$)**: Statistical power is insufficient to detect sub-10 basis point expectancy differentials.
2. **Short History Duration on 15m (10.4 days)**: Must be extended to multi-month historical data.
3. **Negative Expectancy Entry Strategy**: The baseline trend-following strategy operates in negative territory; exit rules modulate drawdown and risk, but cannot transform an unprofitable entry engine into a profitable system.
